**iSNARE** (_Improving SNAREopathy Perspectives_) is an international consortium that brings together basic researchers, clinicians, drug-development groups and patient organisations to develop and validate new treatments for SNAREopathies.

SNAREopathies are rare neurodevelopmental disorders caused by mutations in eight genes that together drive the secretion of chemical signals in the brain. Within 20 years the recognised incidence rose from zero to roughly 1 in 30,000, making SNAREopathies one of the most prevalent rare diseases to date. Treatment options are still limited to controlling symptoms such as seizures, which leaves a phenomenal unmet need.

iSNARE combines cellular assays based on iPSC-derived SNAREopathy patient neurons with in vivo assays using validated SNAREopathy mouse models. Both are embedded in a standardised, integrated framework that systematically tests and compares candidate treatments, and feeds the results back to the treatment sites connected to the consortium.

## Objectives

iSNARE has defined three objectives:

1. Develop novel therapies for SNAREopathies through cell and animal model studies and in silico modeling.
2. Develop predictive biomarkers correlated to therapy efficiency.
3. Replicate four published pre-clinical studies to increase their validity.

## Approach

Candidate treatments are tested in three phases of development:

1. Four small molecules currently tested off-label in small patient cohorts (replication study).
2. Six to ten emerging candidates (small molecules and antisense oligonucleotides).
3. Novel compounds designed de novo by iSNARE in silico modeling using validated SNARE protein templates.

The work is organised into five work packages — coordination and integration (WP1), in silico design (WP2), in vitro tests (WP3), in vivo tests (WP4) and data analysis (WP5). Each work package has its own page under [project](/projects/).
